# Evidence and limits

Evidence snapshot: 21 September 2026. This is a targeted multi-investigator review, not a systematic review, a detector benchmark, or a validated intervention trial. Empirical results, editorial opinions and our recommendations are different categories. Sources were opened by the parent or independent investigators on that date. Abstract-only inspection is identified below; it does not establish replication or full-method review. Living pages may change.

## What the evidence supports

**Distribution shifts exist; individual attribution is a different inference.** S02 studies English PubMed abstracts from 2010–2024. Its 13.5% lower-bound estimate for LLM-processed 2024 abstracts is a corpus-level inference under counterfactual-trend assumptions. The Discussion explicitly says it cannot identify individual processed abstracts, distinguish different LLMs, or separate direct usage from human vocabulary adoption. S01 reports marker turnover. Neither licenses a word blacklist or measures bad writing.

**Polishing can homogenize some dimensions of style.** S06 reports reduced writing-complexity variance across its tasks and models, not a universal loss of all creativity or a requirement to vary sentence lengths artificially. Preserving voice and limiting gratuitous rewrites are editorial responses, not remedies experimentally validated by that study.

**Detection is neither uniformly impossible nor safely universal.** S12 finds highly accurate expert majority judgments in a defined English-article task. S11 finds detector scores confounded by professional editing, in different directions for different detectors. S16 concerns a small academic/mixed-authorship benchmark with consequential score thresholds. These settings and metrics cannot be pooled into one 'detector accuracy'. No detector score is an editing success metric here.

**Surface cleanup can miss substantive problems.** S05 explicitly warns that visible signs can conceal deeper problems. S09 distinguishes a real citation from one that supports the attached claim. S08 and S10 argue for deliberate rhetoric rather than blanket removal. Their editorial advice is useful without treating every causal theory in those essays as established science.

**Language transfer needs evidence.** S14 evaluates French and Chinese output; S03 adapts corpus methods to Korean morphology with Vietnamese comparison. These are reasons not to universalize English features, not validation for all languages or this checklist.

## Source register

Every row gives the material actually inspected. A source opened by an investigator but not reopened by the parent is identified as branch-checked. Numbers quoted below are the papers' reported results, not independently reproduced estimates.

- **S01.** Mingmeng Geng and Roberto Trotta. *Human-LLM Coevolution: Evidence from Academic Writing*. Findings of ACL, July 2025. [Primary record](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.657/). Parent checked abstract and metadata. Statistical arXiv corpus study: conspicuous words such as 'delve' declined while other words increased. Authors infer adaptation; the observation does not isolate its mechanism.
- **S02.** Dmitry Kobak, Rita González-Márquez, Emőke-Ágnes Horvát and Jan Lause. *Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary*. Science Advances, 2 July 2025; arXiv v5, 3 July 2025, first preprint June 2024. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813). Parent read Results and Discussion; no replication. Corpus-level observational evidence, not authorship proof.
- **S03.** Aron Lee. *An LLM-Associated Register Shift in Korean Journal Abstracts: A Morphology-Aware Excess-Vocabulary Study, 2018–2026*. 7 September 2026, v1 working paper. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07447). Parent checked abstract only. Recent cross-language lead with scenario-dependent prevalence; full methods not audited. No language rules are inferred from its percentages.
- **S04.** Simon Willison. *Em dash*. 15 February 2026. [Original note](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/15/em-dashes/). Parent read complete note. Author reports his blog's dash substitution predates LLMs, at least 2015. A concrete counterexample to punctuation-based accusation, not prevalence evidence.
- **S05.** WikiProject AI Cleanup contributors. *Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing*. Living advice page, accessed 21 September 2026. [Original community guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing). Parent read introduction, caveats and early content sections. The page says it is descriptive, not prescriptive, and not Wikipedia policy; its August 2026 banner requests updates for recent models. Domain-specific community experience, not a universal detector or causal account.
- **S06.** Zhivar Sourati, Farzan Karimi-Malekabadi, Meltem Ozcan, Colin McDaniel, Alireza Ziabari, Jackson Trager, Ala Tak, Meng Chen, Fred Morstatter and Morteza Dehghani. *The Shrinking Landscape of Linguistic Diversity in the Age of Large Language Models*. arXiv v2, 24 August 2026; record identifies Nature Human Behaviour publication in 2026. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11266v2), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02550-0). Parent checked abstract/metadata; science branch reports full-text extraction. Three studies, seven datasets, over 880,000 texts; reported 21–50% reduction in writing-complexity variance. Exact API snapshots not independently audited; not a claim about every contemporary model.
- **S07.** Peiqi Sui. *LLMs Exhibit Significantly Lower Uncertainty in Creative Writing Than Professional Writers*. 18 February 2026, v1 preprint. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16162). Parent checked abstract. Analysis of 28 LLMs reports an information-theoretic uncertainty gap. Literary ambiguity is not the same construct as epistemic hedging in a scientific claim; this source must not be used to remove factual safeguards or claim all alignment harms creativity.
- **S08.** Ruth Starkman. *Model Style Is So Cringe*. 15 March 2026. [Original essay](https://ruth.substack.com/p/model-style-is-so-cringe). Parent read full essay. Classroom examples, including international students, and legitimate uses of dashes, triads and contrast. Her question 'whether the sentence needs the device' informs our diagnostics. Anecdotes and causal interpretations are not a prevalence or mechanism experiment.
- **S09.** Jill Walker Rettberg. *LLMs are metaphor machines: orbital argumentation, misaligned citations, and scientific fabrication in AI-generated writing*. AI & Society, Open Forum, 8 September 2026. [Article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-026-03310-4). Parent checked abstract and introduction; qualitative close-reading/theoretical argument. 'Orbital argumentation' approaches but never explains the point; misaligned citations are real works not supporting the specific claim. The proposed metaphor-based mechanism remains the author's theory, not established by this package.
- **S10.** Colin Gorrie. *Why ChatGPT writes like that*. 9 July 2025. [Original essay and visible discussion](https://www.deadlanguagesociety.com/p/rhetorical-analysis-ai). Parent read essay and visible comments. Rhetorical analysis of parallelism, antithesis and tricolon with historical counterexamples. Footnote 6 identifies Claude Sonnet 4 and the letter prompt, which explicitly permits invented details. Anecdotal, not a model-wide benchmark. Visible commenter R. H. Snow (9 July 2025) objects to surrendering human rhetorical devices; one comment is not community consensus.
- **S11.** Hyeonchu Park, Gahye Jeong and Bugeun Kim. *Style as a Confound: False Positives in AI Detection of Non-Native Academic Writing*. arXiv v1, 27 August 2026. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26710). Parent checked abstract. 135,389 professional-editing document pairs, 2018–2025, 13 detectors; the same edits move scores in different directions. Record says 'Presented in EMNLP 2026'; venue/review status not independently certified here. Corpus limits and authors' explicit incomplete separation of origin/style remain.
- **S12.** Jenna Russell, Marzena Karpinska and Mohit Iyyer. *People who frequently use ChatGPT for writing tasks are accurate and robust detectors of AI-generated text*. ACL 2025, arXiv v2, 19 May 2025. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15654v2). Parent checked abstract/metadata. Majority vote of five selected frequent users misclassified one of 300 non-fiction English articles. Tested generator labels include GPT-4o, Claude and o1; exact Claude snapshot not established in the abstract. This is bounded counterevidence to blanket impossibility, not authority to accuse an arbitrary writer.
- **S13.** Google developer documentation style-guide team. *Write for a global audience*. Living first-party guide, observed update 25 August 2026. [Guide](https://developers.google.com/style/translation). Domain branch read full guide. Consistency, helper words and repeated terminology improve comprehension/translation in developer docs. Editorial guidance, not controlled evidence about all prose.
- **S14.** Yanzhu Guo, Simone Conia, Zelin Zhou, Min Li, Saloni Potdar and Henry Xiao. *Do Large Language Models have an English Accent? Evaluating and Improving the Naturalness of Multilingual LLMs*. ACL, July 2025. [Primary record](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.193/). Parent checked abstract and metadata. Corpus-level lexical/syntactic study of French and Chinese naturalness; not universal multilingual coverage.
- **S15.** Kate Niederhoffer, Gabriella Rosen Kellerman, Angela Lee, Alex Liebscher, Kristina Rapuano and Jeffrey T. Hancock. *AI-Generated Workslop Is Destroying Productivity*. HBR, 22 September 2025, updated 25 September. [Article](https://hbr.org/2025/09/ai-generated-workslop-is-destroying-productivity). Domain branch saw summary/introduction/bylines, not full survey. Use only the framing of polished but substantively inadequate workplace output. No survey cost or prevalence numbers adopted.
- **S16.** Mohammad Hadra, Karleen Cambridge and Mostefa Mesbah. *Evaluating the accuracy and reliability of AI content detectors in academic contexts*. International Journal for Educational Integrity, 2 February 2026. [Article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40979-026-00213-1). Science branch checked abstract/methodology. 192 texts including constructed hybrid samples; GPT-4.1 and Claude 3 Opus, experiments January–May 2025. Score bins 0–20/21–79/≥80 operationalize Human/Hybrid/AI. Results do not prove scores measure the percentage of AI authorship.
- **S17.** Mingjie Sun, Yida Yin, Zhiqiu Xu, J. Zico Kolter and Zhuang Liu. *Idiosyncrasies in Large Language Models*. ICML 2025, arXiv v2, 16 June 2025. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12150v2). Science branch checked abstract. Reports 97.1% five-generator validation accuracy with trained embeddings. Model-source classification is not human/AI detection; exact checkpoints and deployment shifts not independently audited here.

## Model-specific observations, kept bounded

The only directly inspected informal output example with an identified version and prompt here is Gorrie's Claude Sonnet 4 letter (S10, July 2025). It illustrates repeated parallelism in one prompt that expressly invited invention; it does not establish that Sonnet 4 always writes this way or that inventing facts is normal in factual editing. Thinking/reasoning mode and complete generation settings are not stated, so the example is not reproducible model-comparison evidence.

S12 names GPT-4o, Claude and o1 for a bounded detection task; S17 reports source-model classification. Neither supplies a reusable brand-to-phrase dictionary. No verified September 2026 fingerprint has been established here for Astra, Luna, Fable, Opus, Kimi, Gemini or Grok. Do not infer one from product names, a subscription tier, a single output, or a model's self-description. Record date, exact model snapshot, prompt, settings/mode, genre and sample before adding such an observation.

## Maintaining this package

Update evidence separately from the compact core. Reopen sources before relying on a live page or a claimed recent model behavior; retain old dated observations as historical. Promote no search-summary claim to checked evidence without opening its source. Different essays repeating one study are not independent empirical replications. Use the smallest supported editorial intervention, then check facts and voice in the actual result.
